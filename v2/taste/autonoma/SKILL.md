# Autonoma

Taste for prose, documents, and presentations. Shared principles:
[autonoma-principles.md](../autonoma-principles.md). This skill is the
writing application.

Change wording, not facts, code, or decisions.

Central question: does the form of this text follow from what it needs
to communicate, who must use it, and the medium it lives in?

Complexity in prose is acceptable when the subject requires it.
Simplicity is valuable when flourish, template sections, or fashionable
structure do not.

## Decision ladder

1. Does this element have a job?
   Remove repetition, filler, ornamental language, speculative detail,
   and sections that exist only because a template has them.

2. Is there already a native form for this kind of document?
   Prefer established structures, terminology, and conventions when they
   express the content adequately.

3. Can the existing material be clarified without new machinery?
   Prefer editing, restructuring, and precise wording before adding
   layers of framing.

4. Is the important structure legible?
   Ownership, status, constraints, dependencies, and “fails when”
   should be findable. Hide incidental noise; do not hide structural
   truth.

5. Only then introduce custom structure or stylistic treatment.
   Add it when it improves comprehension, correct use, or the fit
   between content and audience.

## Text

Start from what the reader must understand or do.

Use language precisely. Prefer concrete words and direct sentences.
Avoid terminology that sounds thorough without a concrete distinction.

Preserve information until there is a reason to hide it. Do not shorten
merely to look concise, and do not expand merely to look complete.
Shorter is wrong when it hides a condition, relationship, or uncertainty
that affects use.

Keep exact commands, paths, API names, class names, statuses, errors,
and quoted or legal text. Do not turn a vague claim into a concrete one.
Flag uncertainty that affects a decision, safety, or credibility.

Cut chatbot filler. When the prose sounds synthetic, scan
[ai-writing-patterns.md](references/ai-writing-patterns.md). Do not add
a personality the source did not have.

A design document, runbook, slide, personal note, and marketing page
should not all sound the same. Prefer the form native to the medium.

## Documents

Structure around purpose. Headings, lists, tables, and paragraphs should
correspond to real distinctions the reader needs.

When the reader must act (a PR, runbook, incident note, or plan), make
status, reason or evidence, next action, and relevant failure modes
findable in one pass. Prefer “because” and “fails when” over abstract
cause words. Do not force that spine on a slide or a personal note.

Consistency is usability: reuse conventions for equivalent concepts;
preserve distinctions that carry meaning.

Optimize for the next reread and the next person who was not in the
room. First-glance polish is a poor test.

## Presentations

One clear idea should dominate each slide. Prefer hierarchy, whitespace,
readable typography, and meaningful visuals over decoration. Do not add
elements merely because the slide feels empty. Completeness of the idea
matters more than the appearance of density or sparseness.

## Before you finish

- Does every significant section or claim have a job?
- Are exact terms unchanged, and is uncertainty still honest?
- Is the important structure legible without a tour?
- Did you keep inconvenient but necessary qualifications?
- Will this remain usable after familiarity replaces novelty?
- Would a careful reader call the form appropriate to the thing?

Solution first. Explain at most three deliberate omissions or
refinements when useful.
