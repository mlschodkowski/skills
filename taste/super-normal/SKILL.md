# Super Normal

Taste for prose, documents, and presentations. Shared principles:
[super-normal-principles.md](../super-normal-principles.md). This skill
is the writing application.

Change wording, not facts, code, or decisions.

Do not optimize for minimality itself. Optimize for the most natural,
appropriate result with the least unnecessary intervention. The best
sentence, section, or slide is often the one that does not need to exist.

Quiet does not mean bland. Personality, craft, rhythm, imagery, humor,
or visual character are welcome when they serve the content and audience
rather than signaling cleverness or authorship.

## Decision ladder

1. Does this need to exist?
   Remove repetition, filler, ornamental language, unnecessary sections,
   speculative detail, and structure that does not help the reader.

2. Is there already an established form that communicates this well?
   Use familiar structures, terminology, conventions, and native formats.

3. Can the existing material simply be clarified?
   Prefer editing, restructuring, and precise wording before adding new
   content.

4. Can the idea be expressed more naturally?
   Prefer plain language, clear hierarchy, concrete wording, and
   appropriate detail.

5. Only then introduce custom structure or stylistic treatment.
   Add it when it genuinely improves comprehension, persuasion, memory,
   usability, or the relationship between the content and its audience.

## Text

Use plain language. Prefer concrete words and direct sentences. Avoid
jargon unless the audience already uses it or the precision is useful.

Remove rhetorical flourishes that draw attention away from the idea.
Preserve useful nuance. Do not simplify merely for the appearance of
simplicity. Shorter is wrong when it gets denser or hides a condition,
relationship, or uncertainty.

Keep exact commands, paths, API names, class names, statuses, errors,
and quoted or legal text. Do not turn a vague claim into a concrete one.
Flag uncertainty that affects a decision, safety, or credibility;
otherwise drop the flourish.

Cut chatbot filler. When the prose sounds synthetic, scan
[ai-writing-patterns.md](references/ai-writing-patterns.md). Do not add
a personality the source did not have.

A technical design document, executive presentation, personal note, and
marketing page should not all sound the same.

## Documents

Use the simplest structure that supports navigation and comprehension.
Prefer familiar headings, lists, tables, paragraphs, and native document
conventions. Do not add sections merely because a template contains them.

When the reader must act (a PR, runbook, incident note, or plan), put
status, evidence or reason, and next action where they can be found in
one pass. Prefer "because" and "fails when" over abstract cause words.
Do not force that spine on a slide, a personal note, or a marketing page.

## Presentations

One clear idea should dominate each slide. Prefer hierarchy, whitespace,
readable typography, and meaningful visuals over decoration. Do not add
animations, diagrams, or visual elements merely because the slide feels
empty. An empty slide can be better than a crowded one; an empty slide
is not automatically better.

## Before you finish

- Could anything be removed without losing useful meaning?
- Are exact terms unchanged, and is uncertainty still honest?
- Is this the natural form for this audience and medium?
- Will this remain useful after the novelty wears off?
- Does the work communicate itself rather than advertise its maker?

Solution first. Explain at most three deliberate omissions or
refinements when useful.
