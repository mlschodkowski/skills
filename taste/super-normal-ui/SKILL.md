# Super Normal UI

Taste for interfaces. Shared principles:
[super-normal-principles.md](../super-normal-principles.md). This skill
is the UI application.

The interface should belong naturally to its category and platform.
Start from interaction patterns people already understand and refine
them only where doing so genuinely improves the experience.

Do not optimize for minimality itself. Optimize for the most natural,
appropriate interface with the least unnecessary intervention.

Quiet does not mean bland. Quality, craft, hierarchy, typography,
spacing, motion, and character are welcome when they arise from the
task, platform, content, and environment.

## Decision ladder

1. Does this element, interaction, animation, or visual treatment need
   to exist?
   Remove decoration, novelty, gamification, gratuitous motion,
   unnecessary controls, and attention-seeking elements.

2. Does the platform already have a familiar solution?
   Use established platform conventions and interaction patterns.

3. Does the category already have an established archetype?
   Start from what users already understand.

4. Can the experience be made clearer without adding another element?
   Prefer hierarchy, spacing, typography, wording, and information
   structure before introducing new controls or visual effects.

5. Only then introduce custom design.
   Make the smallest appropriate improvement that genuinely improves
   comprehension, interaction, accessibility, or atmosphere.

## Working notes

Prefer recognition over discovery. Prefer familiar interaction over
clever interaction. Prefer hierarchy over decoration. Prefer typography
and spacing over visual effects. Prefer stillness over gratuitous
animation. Prefer direct manipulation over unnecessary interaction
layers.

Do not add "delight" merely to demonstrate design skill. Do not make
users learn a new interaction when an established one works. Do not
remove useful elements merely because they are visually prominent.

The goal is not to make the interface invisible. The goal is for the
interface to disappear behind the user's task.

Consider the relationship between the interface, its content,
surrounding screens, device, platform, environment, and frequency of
use. Judge the design by repeated use rather than first-glance impact.

## Before you finish

- Would an experienced designer remove or simplify anything?
- Does every visible element earn its attention?
- Does the interaction belong naturally to its platform and category?
- Can users understand it without learning an invented convention?
- Will this still feel right after months of repeated use?
- Does the interface support the task rather than asserting itself?

Solution first. Explain at most three deliberate omissions or
refinements when useful.
