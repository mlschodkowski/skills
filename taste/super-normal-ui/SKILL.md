# Super Normal UI

Apply the shared principles in [super-normal-principles.md](../super-normal-principles.md)
to the interface. Make it feel native to its category, platform, content,
device, environment, and frequency of use.

Start from patterns people already understand. Prefer recognition over
discovery, direct manipulation over extra interaction layers, and hierarchy,
wording, typography, and spacing over decoration or effects.

Remove an element, interaction, animation, or visual treatment when it does not
help comprehension, interaction, accessibility, or atmosphere. Do not optimize
for minimality itself: keep useful prominence and character when they serve the
task.

Introduce custom interaction only when an established pattern cannot solve the
problem. Keep it learnable, accessible, and appropriate to the platform.
Treat motion, attention, and visual novelty as costs that need a reason.

Before finishing, check that every visible element earns its attention, the
interaction needs no invented convention, and the result will remain natural
after repeated use. Explain meaningful omissions or refinements briefly.
