# Plain Writing Standard

Apply this standard whenever a skill creates or rewrites reader-facing prose. Keep exact quotes, commands, identifiers, paths, error text, and terms whose precision matters.

1. Do not use a familiar metaphor, simile, or other figure of speech.
2. Use a short word when it says the same thing as a long one.
3. Cut every word that adds no meaning.
4. Use active voice unless the passive is clearer or hides an unknown actor.
5. Use everyday English instead of foreign phrases, scientific terms, or jargon when it keeps the meaning.
6. Break a rule when following it would make the writing crude, unclear, false, or unlike the writer's voice.

Read once more before finishing. Keep the facts, the audience's needed terms, and the intended tone; remove only what makes the text harder to read.
